THE CORE IDEA

You need three outfits, not one

Most first-time racers plan for the run and forget the two hours around it. Split race morning into three separate outfit problems and each one gets much easier to solve.

№1

The throwaway layer

An old hoodie or bin bag worn at the start corral, ditched right before the gun. Keeps you warm without warming up your actual race kit.

№2

The race outfit

Only what you'll wear for the next few hours of moving. Lighter than feels sensible while standing still — you'll warm up fast once you're going.

№3

The change bag

Dry clothes waiting at the finish. Wet, salt-stiff race kit gets uncomfortable fast the moment you stop moving and cool down.

HOUR BY HOUR

A typical race morning, layer by layer

Times shift depending on your race, but the sequence of what comes off and when stays roughly the same.

−2h

Wake & travel

Wear the throwaway layer over your race outfit. It's easier to dress once at home than to change at a crowded venue.

−45m

Bag drop

Hand off your change bag before you queue for the start corral — bag trucks usually close well before the gun.

−15m

Corral wait

Standing still in the cold is the coldest part of your whole morning. Keep the throwaway layer on until the last possible minute.

0

Gun time

Drop the throwaway layer to the side — most races collect and donate them. You're now in race kit only.

+2–4h

On the course

Sun, wind, and effort all change how warm your kit feels versus how it felt standing at the start line.

Finish

The crash

Body temperature drops fast once you stop. Dry layers from your change bag go on within minutes, not after the medal photos.

SPECIFICS

Two details worth getting right

Small choices that separate a comfortable race from a distracting one.

BIB PLACEMENT

Pin it flat, pin it early

A bib pinned the night before saves fumbling in a crowded corral. Pin through all four corners so it doesn't flap, fold, or block the timing strip on the back.

  • Pin over your race outfit, not the throwaway layer
  • Keep it flat — a folded bib can miss timing mats
  • Skip pinning it to a jacket you plan to remove

CARRYING ESSENTIALS

Pockets over pouches

A running belt or built-in pockets beat carrying anything by hand for hours. Keep gels, a phone, and cash for after — a bulky waist pouch tends to bounce and chafe by the halfway mark.

  • Test any belt or pack on a training run first
  • Keep pockets accessible without stopping
  • Store finish-line ID or cash somewhere waterproof

LEARNED THE HARD WAY

Common race-day outfit mistakes

Nearly all of these come from testing something for the first time on race morning instead of in training.

Skip this

  • Wearing brand-new shoes for the first time on race day
  • Dressing for how the start corral feels, not the whole race
  • Cotton anything — it stays wet and heavy once it's soaked
  • New socks straight out of the packet, unwashed
  • Forgetting a hat or cap when the forecast says sun

Do this instead

  • Run at least one long training session in full race kit
  • Dress about 10° warmer than the corral actually feels
  • Pick synthetic or merino fabric, tested on a prior run
  • Wear socks that have already survived a wash cycle
  • Pack layers you can remove, not just ones you can add
